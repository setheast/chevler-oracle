## Rulings

1. **Base labour = £20.29/hr** (grade-3 operator, fully loaded), not S7's £19.33. Lewys builds in headroom (the Zhen standard assumes 5 running; the real average is 3–4). Every machine rate on that base rises by £0.96 × crew. Other labour grades (£25.69 slitter/guillotine, £23.91 bench) are not yet confirmed. → **R3 resolved.**
2. **CHEVS crew includes a shared packer**: the New Chevler ×(2÷1.5) uplift is correct (one packer serves three Chevlers). In Oracle, crew is explicit — model the CHEVS station as 1.5 operators + a third of a packer per machine, or the equivalent crew factor; do not drop it. → **R9 resolved.**
3. **Press set-up at the model rates is right** (£58.30 Wide / £51.17 Narrow, not £35.79): tonnage has fallen, so per-unit costs rose.
4. **One efficiency rate per machine, from the productivity reports** (timesheets, 9 months of data; 10RF ≈ 93%), replacing the model's 85% + the quotes' 75%. Historic efficiencies were invented. → **R4 direction set**: the productivity factor is the single efficiency, sourced from timesheet data, per machine.
5. **Running hours need reviewing across all machines and shift patterns** (10RF now carries 8RF's work on the model's 1,377 hours); rates refresh from next year's budget once Kelsey accepts it. → feeds **R1**.
6. **S&A stays 15.5%** (reforecast 15.6%; range 15–16%). The *method* is open: Lewys prefers spreading it through machine-hour rates; Seth prefers a fixed per-order charge + a small value-linked %. Both agree a flat % of price overcharges big jobs. S&A also holds insurance and IT. → **R12 refined.**
7. **The 4.5% carriage recovers its pot** (~£304k) — and the pot is wider than freight: storage, dispatch handling and consumables, sales rebates, and shipment charges invoiced to customers. When delivery becomes a per-job line, the rebates and other non-freight parts still need recovering somewhere.
8. **Delivery becomes its own quote line: PlasPac rate cards (pallets × zone) + ~15% margin** for fuel-surcharge movement between rate-card updates; the invoicing team (Sue, Sharon, Steph) needs instructions and current rate cards. → **R10: delivery margin seeded at 15%.**
9. **Bad-debt allowance: £50,000 a year** (Series, K-Kad, Ermefact exposure; historic write-offs only £600 in 7 years). The sandbox prices it as a named share of the selling price, 0.0074 (= £50k ÷ ~£6.76M revenue, inferred — confirm). In Oracle it is a named, dated, owned parameter, deducted in NP, and excluded from any legacy-comparison baseline.
10. **The £25 Cases admin fee** is probably Stuart's quoting time — review it alongside the S&A method.

## Sandbox implementation to port

- `badDebtPct` — params in both stores; the gross-up's fourth share (`engines/main/shared.ts` `grossUp(…, badDebtPct)`, `engines/cases/shared.ts` column `badDebtPct`); every NP calculation subtracts it; the workbook baselines force 0.
- `server/cases-workcentres-seed.ts` — the S7 rates re-based to £20.29 labour.

## Pattern worth keeping

**Every pricing change runs against a frozen legacy baseline** (Seth, 15 Oct 2026: "as long as I can still see what the old workbook model produces… I need to keep an eye on price drift"). The sandbox shows "Compare with workbook price" on every estimate and prints it on the internal PDF; each new parameter is forced to its workbook value (usually 0) inside the baseline so its effect shows as drift. Oracle's parallel-running phase (spec §6) should carry the same per-quote drift view.
