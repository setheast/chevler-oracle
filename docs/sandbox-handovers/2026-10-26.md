## Rulings

1. **Engineering is inside the machine-hour rate — no engineer premium anywhere** (Lewys, 26 Oct). His factory-overhead budget recovers engineering labour (£226,730, by machine running hours) and engineering (£160,200, on history) in the hourly rate. A separate engineer £/hr on make-ready double counts. The workbook's "make-ready = machine + £29.50 engineer" is superseded; in Oracle make-ready is costed at the machine's own rate. → **new R17** (nine legacy sandbox rows still carry it; Seth deciding).
2. **Crew by head count** (Seth, 23 Oct; James, 26 Oct): a twin-head machine/tool needs ONE packer; a three-head needs TWO, so 3-head running is normally counter-productive (1.5× output for 2× labour). **9REEL is a triple head with two operators**; 10REEL and 11REEL are twin-head Snyders; 9REEL is not a Snyder. Oracle's Machines entity carries heads and crew explicitly, and crew scales with the head configuration fitted.
3. **No finance on any reel-fed machine; depreciation belongs in the rate** (Seth, 23 Oct). In Lewys's budget only the flow wrapper carries finance (£12,314).
4. **11REEL is idle** — commissioned for a customer's orders since lost (Seth, 23 Oct); no timesheet rows after June 2026. Its 8REEL-class proxy (£78.37) is agreed too high by Seth and Lewys. **Policy question for Oracle:** a full-absorption rate per running hour overprices exactly the work you would want to load onto an idle machine — how quotes price work onto spare capacity must be decided (Lewys/Seth), not left to the rate. Worked example: 57 × 48 printed, 14.4m — 5REEL single (£11.47/1,000) vs 11REEL twin tool (£11.50); break-even 11REEL rate £77.48/hr.
5. **Lewys's overhead budget is the template for computing machine rates** (sent 26 Oct; full table in production-knowledge §21): 28 lines, £1,922,087, each with a recovery type — space £338,901, machine running hours £358,450, labour hours £325,026, kWh £216,000, history £177,000, waste tonnage £77,520, machine-specific depreciation £71,114, consumables £42,000, Chevs gas £12,250, and **packs produced £303,826 = the carriage/storage/customs pot** (exactly the ~£304k stripped from the S7 rates). **For Oracle:** hold machine rates as a CALCULATION in the calibration module — budget line × recovery share (per-machine drivers: m², running hours, kWh, crew, history split) ÷ running hours + crew × base labour — never as typed numbers, so a change in hours or budget re-rates every machine visibly. The drivers are requested from Lewys. → **new R18.**
6. **Lewys will rebuild the model after the redundancy costs are confirmed** (engineering, cleaning and salary absorption all change), adding 10 and 11 and removing 8RF, and bring Access into line. → **R1/R2 timing.**
7. **Allocation choices to revisit on a blank canvas** (Lewys): the general hands' indirect element is spread over the reel formers only "because it historically was"; depreciation sits on floor space; "history" splits are opaque; site expenses go by waste tonnage although tonnage in/out is not measured. Oracle should make each recovery basis an explicit, owned choice.
8. **VP-16 is decommissioned** (Seth, 23 Oct) and **8RF dismantled** (Stuart, 14 Oct). The sandbox holds `DECOMMISSIONED_MACHINES` and raises an advisory quote check; Oracle's Machines carry an active/historical status — historical machines keep their rates for re-pricing old quotes but are never routable. → **R16 extended.**
9. **Reference-only dies** (Seth, 23 Oct; S9 = Stuart's 2013 crimped-case catalogue): "does a die exist for this size" matters but the list has no die references, heads, speeds or webs. The sandbox imports it as `dataStatus: "reference"` tools — searchable, flagged, never fitted or auto-fitted until enriched (fits-machines + heads + cycles, then "ready for quoting"); a size already on file is skipped unless Stuart tags another machine family (a probable second die — 17 such). **For Oracle:** a Tool has a data-confidence level; reference tools prove existence only and can't drive a routing. Also: a blank "fits machines" must never mean "fits any" for a low-confidence tool. → **R7 extended.**
10. **Printed blank +12mm?** Stuart's 2013 sheet sizes printed blanks with +12mm against our 6mm Reel Fed trim. Unconfirmed — question for Stuart.
11. **Evidence of under-pricing on small cases:** YF944 (38 × 29, Spiral Packs) costs £2.47 per 1,000 on Lewys's figures and sells at £2.19–£2.23; Stuart says Papertech sets the market with multi-head Snyders. Efficiency is the biggest lever (standards ~80% vs ~60% achieved). Run-speed standards are tribal knowledge (Stuart, Hannah, James, Tim) and must be captured — a CHEVS job moved ~£10/1,000 between 4 and 5 cycles/min.
12. **Resilience requirement** (Seth + Lewys, 26 Oct): the online system needs **daily backups and continued access if the internet goes down** — today's on-premises server gives both, and that must not be lost. Also: one estimator on one principle, one source of truth replacing Access + Excel + the three calculators.
