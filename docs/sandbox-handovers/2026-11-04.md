## Rulings

1. **Sheet-fed round cases: the flat blank is base + 2 × wall** (D1 + 2 · D2), the same reading as Reel Fed (Seth, 3 Nov 2026). Punched round blanks sit in a **square grid or staggered rows** (alternate rows offset half a blank); the die decides which. Evidenced: one or the other reproduces 172 of the 257 sheet-fed round cases in Access.

2. **How round blanks are punched** (Stuart, 4 Nov, S15):
   - **The cutter:** a forged-steel ring cutter, one per size, cutting one blank per stroke through a stack of up to 500 sheets.
   - **Layout:** the operator places it blank by blank, staggering the rows to get the most from the sheet.
   - **2-up:** a 2-up cutter needs twice the pressure, so it is self-defeating on today's presses.
   - **Cutting boards:** used only where a crease is needed, about 20–30 sheets at a time.

3. **A gap between blanks and at the sheet edges** (Seth, 4 Nov):
   - standard **10mm**, operators' minimum **6mm**;
   - a layout that only fits between the two is "tight": shown, never applied automatically;
   - it's a setting on the punch work centre, not a constant.

4. **Punch presses** (Gaynor Morgan, 4 Nov, S16):

   | Press | Max sheet | Sheets per cut |
   |---|---|---|
   | Travel head | 610 × 1,000 (set by the board and interleaving table) | 500 |
   | Clickers | 500 × 900 | 500, or 334 |

   The board machine is a separate thing. **For Oracle (Seth):** each machine carries its own table of bespoke fields (blank gap, max sheet, head type, sheets per cut, …), and a work centre can have several physical machines (punch presses, the Zhens), because planning needs to know which job runs on which.

5. **Reel-Sheet and Sheeter limits** (Seth, 3–4 Nov):
   - **Reel-Sheet:** takes **526mm max** (275 min). It cuts **only at its cylinder repeats** (197, 275, 297, 305, 320, 330, 348, 354, 425, 489mm), so 489 is the longest printed sheet. A printed sheet job from a wider mother is **pre-slit, and the slitting is costed**.
   - **Sheeter:** takes 500–1,325mm and **slits inline on plain jobs**, so there's no separate slitting charge.

6. **CHEVS crew uplift is labour only** (Seth, 4 Nov). CHEVS = machine £20.94 + 2 × £20.29 = **£61.52/hr**. The workbook's ×(2 ÷ 1.5) on the whole rate (£68.50) also scaled the machine part, which was wrong. Every Chev needs a feeder at the top and a packer at the bottom. A shared packer only lowers the crew when one packer packs for more than one Chev, which belongs in the crew/planning model (not built). **Supersedes R9's 15 Oct reading.**

7. **Conversion follows the operation chosen** (Seth, 4 Nov). On New Chevler: Sheeter £0.26/kg plain, Reel-Sheet £1.33/kg printed, not the template's flat £0.40/kg.

8. **Every process is costed from its real steps** (Seth, 4 Nov). Punching, for example:
   - sheets per lift (≤ 500), blanks per sheet, strokes = lifts × blanks per sheet (30k ÷ 500 = 60 strokes);
   - minutes per stroke, lift handling, set-up;
   - gaps, edge margin, max sheet and head type.

   Today punching is a flat typed run factor (PUNCH 47 = 47,000 blanks an hour). This is a requirement for Oracle's step costing, not something to port.

9. **Amortised tooling and print origination are charged at cost** (Seth, 4 Nov): no NP, S&A, carriage, bad debt or uplift. Price = (factory − at-cost £) ÷ divisor + at-cost £, and the margins are worked on the price less them.

10. **Packing on the customer quote is a per-job switch** (Seth, 4 Nov: "many occasions where these numbers are unconfirmed"). It overrides the certainty bar either way, and the choice is recorded on the quote.

11. **A sheet size chosen for a printed job fills the print origination** (Seth, 4 Nov): the press, plate and colours are known from the route.

12. **Proven before price** (Seth, 4 Nov, on WWPK04; §34): "Proven running records should come before price p/1k where that info is available."
    - **What counts:** a machine with a proven running record, first the product's own history (Confirmed), else the same size and paper weight (Evidenced).
    - **Recency:** last ordered within 3 years.
    - **Retired machines:** 8REEL history counts as 10REEL; VP-16 never counts.
    - **The recommendation:** the cheapest route on that machine, even when an unproven machine prices cheaper. The cheaper one stays listed with what staying proven costs.
    - **Not a price change:** only which route is recommended and auto-applied.

13. **Currency while pricing** (Seth, 4 Nov). The estimator shows the pricing in £, $ or € with one click, at **the same rate the quote will use** (ECB × (1 + margin), 4dp). Display only: GBP stays the price of record. **Open for Oracle:** customers who buy in € under forward contracts. Woodland's 5th contract asks for delivery on 5 Jul 2027 at a fixed € price, and we can't hold a price that long (Seth). Price-hold and currency-of-record policy: Sharon's steer requested (R29).

14. **Trunking to the 3PL isn't costed** (Stuart spotted it, 4 Nov). The New delivery method prices 3PL storage, handling and picking and the customer delivery, but **not moving the goods from Hengoed to PlasPac**, which sat inside the old 4.5%. It needs a per-pallet trunking rate in the fulfilment cost. Lewys has been asked how the goods get there and what it costs (R26).

15. **Storage dwell for forward-dated stock** (Seth, 4 Nov). 4 weeks at the 3PL is an assumption.
    - **Hannah:** real dwell by product type, and when forward-dated contract stock is made.
    - **Lewys:** average pallets in store per week from the invoices.

    A job made now for July 2027 would sit about 34 weeks (R27).

## Addendum (4 Nov 2026, late): sales history is reference data, and the Sales Explorer is a view Oracle must have

**Ruling (Seth, 4 Nov 2026):** the invoiced sales history behind the **Chevler Sales Explorer** artifact is "golden for day to day referencing". It is "great to be able to look up customers' previous/current prices as a reference point when quoting". Today's Woodland job shows the gap: its last price paid (€151.62 a box) came from Sharon's email, not from the system.

**What the Explorer holds today:**
- **Data:** invoiced sales from Sage, January 2019 to 30 Oct 2026, with credits netted and stock movements excluded. The financial year runs December to November.
- **Analysis:**
  - by customer, product, category (Sage board group, report line and sub-category) and product details (paper type, colour, product type, plain or printed, size);
  - by machine, country and customer type;
  - measured in £ net, packs or units.
- **Views:** Explorer, Watchlist, Customer types, Budget and Quote helper; saved views; customer types curated by hand by Seth (with a confidence level).

**For Oracle:**
1. **A Sales History plane** of reference data: invoice lines (customer, product, date, quantity, packs, units, net £, currency, credit links). Read-only, with source and date recorded. Customer type and other curated attributes are Oracle-owned and audited.
2. **Quoting step 0 reads it:** for the customer and product (or similar products), the last price paid, the current price, volumes and order frequency, shown as reference beside the new quote.
3. **The Sales Explorer becomes a reporting view over the spine,** carrying across its views and the curated customer types.
4. **Until Oracle is the live system, Seth refreshes it by uploading files:** Excel sheets, Access exports and Sage data. Oracle therefore needs an **import pipeline** that:
   - records the source, period and date of each upload, and who uploaded it;
   - validates the file before applying it;
   - can be re-run safely without duplicating (replace by period, or merge by invoice number);
   - shows what changed and can be rolled back.

   Sales data is commercially sensitive, so access is controlled server-side.

**R30 (new): sales history import.** Open questions:

- how credits are linked;
- currency (€ invoices vs the GBP of record);
