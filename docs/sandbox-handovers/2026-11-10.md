1. **A running Chev is 1.5 operators** (Lewys, by email, passed on 9 Nov; §35).

2. **The Slitter and Reel-Sheet rates stay as they are until Lewys rebuilds the cost model after the consultation** (§35). That means the Slitter at £0.41/kg (S7 SLITT £45.18/hr on record) and the Reel-Sheet at £1.33/kg. Lewys says the Reel-Sheet depends on the active tonnage through each machine, which is "extremely difficult to compute on our existing system". Both are calibration items, not truths (R22).

3. **Quotes assume 48 hours at the 3PL** (Sharon, Sales Manager, 9 Nov; §36).
   - **Who sits longer:** most products are called off within 48 hours. Stock products sit 4–6 weeks, but they are priced from the price list, not quoted.
   - **Default:** dwell = 48 ÷ 168 = **0.2857 weeks**. It replaces the 4-week assumption, and a quote can still type its own (e.g. forward-dated contract stock).
   - **Ruling 66 stands:** dwell is per job. This only sets the default.
   - **Sandbox:** the `plaspac-rates-seed.ts` policy, and fractional-week inputs.

4. **3PL storage is billed per week started** (David Watkins, PlasPac, by email, 9 Nov; §37): "if a pallet is in stock any time during the week it is charged the full weekly rate".
   - A 48-hour stay therefore pays **one full week** (£1.50 a pallet space; £1.75 from 1 Dec 2026).
   - A stay that crosses PlasPac's week boundary pays two. We price one, the usual case.
   - **For Oracle:** the fulfilment model takes a **storage billing basis** (`weekStarted` vs pro rata). Billed weeks = the dwell rounded up, at least one.

5. **Trunking Hengoed → PlasPac is £150 per collection**, a full artic load collected from our site (David Watkins, §37).
   - **Assumed, unconfirmed:** 26 UK pallets a collection (a single-stacked 13.6m trailer), so **£5.77 a pallet space**, charged per pallet in the fulfilment cost next to RH&D.
   - **Per pallet space on a quoted job:** £1.50 storage + £2.50 RH&D + £5.77 trunking = **£9.77**.
   - **Settles ruling 65's "not yet costed"**; R26 becomes R33 (how full the collections really are).
   - **Sandbox:** `fulfilment.ts` `billedStorageWeeks`, `trunkingPerPallet`, policy fields `storageBilling`, `trunkingPerCollection`, `palletsPerCollection`. Missing fields = pro rata and no trunking, so older quotes reproduce.

6. **The route comparison shows what each machine has actually run of this size** (Seth, 9 Nov: "it would appear that 1RF is the proven option but it does not show that in the Route list").
   - **What it lists:** per machine, the same-size products of the last 3 years at any paper weight, with orders, paper weights, last order and the speed it ran at (strokes/min ÷ die heads).
   - **When nothing is proven:** it says why, e.g. "this size has run on 1REEL at 39 and 50gsm, not on this weight".
   - **Also on 9 Nov:**
     - the comparison is sorted cheapest first;
     - it shows output/hr, £/hr, offcut, set-up £/order, the factory-cost split (material / machine / set-up per 1,000) and the difference from the recommended route;
     - "how we've run similar products" is ranked by best fit (exact size, then paper weight), then **most recent order**.
   - **For Oracle:** the route step shows the run record beside the cost, and the similar-products reference is recency-first.

7. **Paper weights within 1gsm are the same weight: 39 = 40** (Seth, 10 Nov; §38).
   - **Why:** a 64 × 38 40gsm Natural GP job found no proven machine and took its packing from WSB07, a 40gsm PK product last ordered in 2017 with a 1,000 placeholder pack. Every recent 64 × 38 GP product is **39gsm**: WAB281 (Jun 2026, 57 orders) and WCLAY01 on 1REEL, and EH360.
   - **Applies to:**
     - the proven machine (ruling 63's "same paper weight");
     - the run history;
     - the similar-products ranking;
     - packing suggestions;
     - recorded Packing Standards, where an exact-weight row beats a ±1 row.
   - **Evidence wording:** it names the other weights used ("incl. 39gsm").
   - **For Oracle:** a named tolerance (`SAME_PAPER_WEIGHT_GSM` = 1) on every "same paper" match. It is a production rule, so it is editable and audited, not a literal.

8. **The pallet build is suggested from history, with a certainty, like the packing** (Seth, 10 Nov; §38).
   - **Why it follows the carton:** units per carton × cartons per pallet follows the carton, and the carton follows the size, paper weight and pack, not the forming machine.
   - **Matching order:**
     1. same size + paper grade + weight;
     2. any paper of that weight;
     3. any weight (× 0.6).
   - Within each tier, products that packed **this job's pack** per carton come first. The pack is the job's own, else the suggested one.
   - **Units per carton:** the pack, where it agrees with the carton's BOM rate within 2%, else the carton's rate.
   - **Weighting and certainty:** orders × recency; certainty = leading share × n ÷ (n + 10).
   - **No product of the size used that pack:** the usual build is offered at half weight and never auto-filled.
   - **Fresh jobs:** auto-fill at the packing bars. Typed and as-run builds are never touched.
   - **Today:**
     - 64 × 38 40gsm GP → 10,000 × 30, C6A, 90%;
     - with a 12,600 pack → 12,600 × 30, 52% (WCLAY01 only);
     - 51 × 38 50gsm GP → 19,800 × 30, ~100%.

9. **Every suggested figure says where it came from, beside the inputs** (Seth, 10 Nov: "a small notes section that explains where the data has come from"). Customer Requirements has a "Where these figures came from" box with one line per nest / sleeve / pack / pallet build. Each line says one of:
   - as run on an Access product;
   - a recorded standard;
   - auto N% with the evidence;
   - matches history;
   - typed (with what history suggests).

   The internal PDF prints the pallet build's source.

   **For Oracle:** every defaulted field on a quote carries a provenance record (source, certainty, evidence). The wizard shows it in plain English at the step where the field is entered, and the blueprint prints it.

## Addendum (10 Nov 2026, afternoon): cycle speeds from run history — Stuart's bands retired

10. **Cycle speeds come from what has actually run** (Seth, 10 Nov; production-knowledge §39): "Stuart's banding is failing us … create our own banding based on what has actually been run."
    - **Why the bands failed:** across 210 recent round-case Reel Fed products, they miss in both directions. 86–100mm blanks run at a median of 40 against the band's 55; 101–125mm at 45 against 40. That's because speed follows the machine and paper weight, not just the blank: 51 × 38 at 65gsm runs 50 on 1REEL and 36 on 2REEL. A 64 × 38, banded 36, has only run 20 on 1REEL.
    - **The rule, per forming machine** (every machine, every template):
      1. the product's own routing;
      2. the same size + machine + weight (±1gsm);
      3. the same size + machine, any weight (× 0.75);
      4. the same size on the machine's group (× 0.5);
      5. sizes within 15% on the machine, a guesstimate (× 0.4).
    - **Which runs count:** the last 3 years' products where there are any.
    - **Speed and certainty:** the order-weighted median; certainty = share within 10% × n ÷ (n + 10) × the tier factor.
    - **At 70%+ it becomes the Standard**; 35–70% is offered.
    - **No history:** the die's or machine's speed, unverified (R14).
    - **Faster than anything this size has run on the machine:** an attention check, never an error.
    - **The basis is consistent:** routing speeds are the set-up standard each product ran at, and the measured efficiencies are attainment against exactly those standards.
    - **For Oracle:** a machine's standard speed for a product is derived from its run record with a confidence, before die ratings or general bands. A general band, if one is ever kept, must never read as a recommendation above proven runs. Superseded: Stuart's bands and the "can't beat gravity" error.
    - **Sandbox:**
      - `server/speed-history.ts` (`speedHistoryMap`);
      - `standardCycle` / `speedHistoryFor` / `speedHistoryKey` / `speedGuide` (`estimate-checks.ts`);
      - `SPEED_HISTORY_AUTO_MIN`, `SPEED_SUGGEST_MIN`, `SPEED_TOLERANCE` (`production-rules.ts`);
      - tests: `server/speed-history.test.ts`.

**R-register:**
- **R8:** superseded. Run history first, then the die's rated speed; the bands are gone.
- **R14:** the run history replaces die ratings wherever a size has run on a machine. Die and default speeds now matter only where it hasn't.
