## Rulings

1. **The route in use is always the top row of the route list** (Seth, 11 Nov 2026). He was looking at a job on its own layout (10REEL 2-up, cheaper than every standard route) and said: "you could be forgiven for thinking that 3REEL is the chosen route".
   - **What went wrong:** the job's own layout wasn't one of the standard routes, so it had no row. The only row shown was the cheapest standard route, and it read as the choice.
   - **Now:**
     - The route in use leads, marked **In use** (an ink left edge as well as the badge).
     - Then come the recommended (★ proven) route and the cheapest; the rest stay behind "Show more".
     - Choosing another route moves it to the top.
     - A job on its own layout gets its own top row ("Your own layout"), priced from the job as entered.
     - The comparison column compares against ★; when nothing is recommended, it compares against the route in use.
   - **Supersedes** ruling 94's "closed, the route list shows the proven route, the cheapest and the job's own".
   - **For Oracle:** whatever the route candidacy returns, the screen must always show which route the job is on, first. A job whose route isn't a standard candidate is still a row.

2. **The estimator is one collapsible section per step, in the job's order** (Seth, 11 Nov 2026). Seth set the order; Claude sense-checked it and Seth agreed the four points below.
   - **The sections:**
     1. Product
     2. Packing
     3. Route, with four steps:
        - Print
        - Machine
        - Tool
        - Material piece & conversion
     4. Price
     5. Delivery
     6. Quote details
     7. Workings & details
   - **The agreed points:**
     - **Print type is a customer requirement and stays in Product.** Route → Print holds how it is printed: the press (in the sandbox, the Conversion field), press web, pre-slit, press speed and origination.
     - **On a plain job** the conversion (Sheeter / Slitter / none) sits in Material piece & conversion, since nothing is printed.
     - **The production history** ("how we've run similar products") belongs inside Route, as the evidence for the route choice.
     - **Workings go last.** They are reference, not a step.
   - **How each section behaves:**
     - It has an icon and a title.
     - Closed, it shows its key facts as pills (e.g. "CHEVS · 3/min standard · Efficiency 62%").
     - A closed section shows any input it still needs in red ("Needs Nest qty", "Needs a delivery option").
     - Open/closed is remembered per user, and a link to a section opens it.
     - Under a route list, the four Route steps start closed; with no route list they stay open.
   - **Supersedes** ruling 94's input order (packing moves out of Product into its own section; workings move to the end). The right-hand summary, the colour roles and "detail behind a click" stand.
   - **Pure layout:** the estimate requests and prices were checked byte-identical before and after on 8 Access loads across every template, a blank form and 3 printed jobs.
   - **For Oracle:** a UX reference, not a spec. The guided flow (§2) should follow the same order. The required-input pills are a cheap way to say what a closed section still needs.

3. **Packing is costed from the sleeve and pack quantities by default** (Seth, 11 Nov 2026). Before, it was costed by weight.
   - **What changed:** new Cases forms and Access loads now start on "Sleeve & pack qty" (`packingBasis: "qty"`). That costs one inner sleeve per sleeve qty plus one outer carton per pack qty, at the Settings £ each. Since 8 Oct the default had been "By weight": product kg per 1,000 × a £/kg per template (the historic medians, never confirmed by Stuart).
   - **Recalled jobs are unaffected:** saved products and quotes keep the basis they were saved with.
   - **Effect, measured on the sandbox's 12 Oct local data at the first quantity:**

     | Product | Price per 1,000 |
     |---|---|
     | WBYN29 | £7.77 → £7.67 (−1.2%) |
     | EW502 | −0.3% |
     | WPAN06 | +1.3% |
     | MW001 | +1.1% |
     | CC002 | −3.1% |

   - **Caveat:** a blank sleeve or pack prices that line at £0. The `packing-missing` check flags it, and packing suggestions fill the quantities at ≥ 50% certainty on fresh jobs.
   - **Sandbox:** `TEMPLATE_CONFIG[*].defaults.packingBasis` in `cases-estimator-app.jsx`, and `casesInputsFromAccess` in `access-product-map.ts`.
   - **For Oracle:** packing is a **bill of packaging components** (sleeve, carton, pallet, each with a stock code and a cost), costed from the job's pack build. A £/kg estimate is a fallback only for when the build is unknown, and must be labelled as an estimate. This matches the S&R gap list in the 4 Nov hand-over, item (d): packaging components have no stock codes yet.

## Addendum — paper, then reel; choosing the reel (Seth, 11 Nov 2026, afternoon)

Live 11 Nov 2026 (Seth tried it locally first). Folded into Oracle as rulings 99–100.

4. **The product is the paper; the reel is the route's choice.**
   - **Ruling (Seth, 11 Nov 2026):** "Ultimately we want the route box to auto choose the best reel width with confidence." On reel choice: "I want it to suggest the cheapest option with the least waste, and does that then match up with what has been previously run — that may not be the best option."
   - **What changed:**
     - **Product asks for the paper** (grade + weight).
     - **The Route chooses the reel** (which stock line / width). The Reel table under the Route box prices every reel of the paper all-in on the route in use.
     - **★ goes to the cheapest.** Within 0.5% (`REEL_NEAR_TIE_PCT`) of it, the reel this size has mostly been made from wins when that history is at least 50% sure (`REEL_HISTORY_MIN_CERTAINTY`); with no such history, the reel with less offcut (`reelNearTie`). **Band study, same day** (213 Reel Fed products ordered in the last 3 years, every reel priced at the last order quantity): near ties are common (48 of 151 within 0.5%); least-offcut-first moved 19 of its 25 changes AWAY from the reel we run for ~0.5 points of offcut; history-first changes 12 of 151 (~£1.36 an order, max £4.32) and matches the run reel on 107 of 151 (from 82). Seth: "Yes" to history-first at 0.5%.
     - **History is shown beside each reel:** which reels this size has been made from (Access BOM mother reel, same size, weight within 1gsm, recent orders count more, certainty = share × n ÷ (n + 10)).
     - **Printed jobs are priced on every reel** of the paper on each press, not one per press. Narrow takes reels ≤ 526mm as they are, and wider reels pre-slit.
   - **Sandbox:**
     - `reelHistory`, `leastWasteNearTie` and the `reel` field on candidates in `route-optimiser.ts`;
     - `printRouteOptions(…, {allReels})` in `print-route.ts`;
     - `paperOptions` / `defaultLineForPaper` in `cases-material-lines.ts`;
     - the `ReelTable` and `PaperPicker` in `cases-estimator-app.jsx`.
     - Tests: `reel-width-choice.test.ts`.
   - **For Oracle:**
     - A Product's spec holds the **material grade + weight** (a material family), never a stock line.
     - The job's routing selects the **stock item** (reel width) by cost with waste, each choice carrying its evidence: the price gap and the history share.
     - The history check is advisory. The cheapest, least-waste reel is the recommendation even when we have usually run another. The UI shows the disagreement, and the user can override.
   - **Decided (Seth, 11 Nov 2026):** 0.5%, history first. **For Lewys:** 54 recent jobs where the cheapest reel isn't the one we've run: 23 by under 2%; 8 where the reel we ran isn't offered now; and 23 by 2% or more — mainly small printed Narrow Reeler jobs where a 516/525mm reel run as it is beats pre-slitting the 1,050 by 15–29% (is the per-job pre-slit cost right, or are narrow webs slit for stock?), and big plain cases on 7/11REEL where a 285/300mm slit reel bought at width beats the mill reel by 8–17% (are the slit-reel prices and stock right?). Oracle: pre-slit cost per job vs slit-for-stock is a costing-model question (R-register).
5. **The route's steps are shown in decision order** (Seth, 11 Nov 2026): Machine → Tool → Material piece & conversion → Print. Production order is a separate view (Structure & Route). For Oracle: the routing editor can follow decision order while the S&R / work order follows operation sequence.

## Addendum 2 — the production history becomes evidence: filters and prices paid (Seth, 11 Nov 2026, evening)

Live 11 Nov 2026. Display only: no price changes.

6. **Prices paid sit beside every similar product** (Seth: "low price, high price and median price … a very good visual steer on what the price is like … a steer on the qtys attached").
   - **Source:** the Sage Line 50 sales history (Seth's Sales History Dataset, `history_line_items.csv`, Jan 2019 – Oct 2026), reduced by `scripts/extract-sales-prices.py` to `[invoice date, units, £ per 1,000, anonymous buyer]` per stock code. Kept: invoices only (credits out), product lines only (no carriage), units = packs × pack size, £ per 1,000 = net ÷ units × 1,000. No customer names or account codes in the extract.
   - **What the estimator shows:** per product, Low / Median / High per 1,000, each with its delivery quantity, plus the invoice count.
   - **The period:** a chosen period counted back from today. The options are 30, 60 or 120 days, or 6, 12, 24 or 36 months; **the default is 12 months**. Seth: "historical data is not much use". Blank when there's none.
   - **The median counts each price agreement (one buyer at one price) once:** a contract invoiced on every call-off out-voted everyone. EW500's 86 invoices were 17 agreements; EC500's median moves from £17.29 to £20.39.
   - **"Median near <qty>":** the same median over invoices from half to twice the job's chosen quantity.
   - **Tried and dropped the same day:** a price-against-quantity chart with the job's quotes and quantity bands (Seth: "I don't like the chart. It doesn't work.").
   - **For Oracle:**
     - Sales invoices are a first-class evidence source next to production history.
     - The Quote screen should show what this product, and like products, actually sold for recently, at a comparable quantity.
     - "Agreement" (customer × price) is the unit for typical price; an invoice is a delivery, not an order. Oracle should link invoices to orders so order size is real, not delivery size.
     - The Sage feed should be automatic rather than an export.
   - **Open (R-register):** order linkage. Call-off contracts can't be told apart from repeat orders without the order reference.
7. **The production history is filterable** with counts that follow the other filters: Code, Material, Machine, Mother reel and Print (search + multi-select) and Strokes/min. Mother reel is its own column; rows are compact and paged 25 at a time. For Oracle: the "similar products" evidence panel needs faceted filtering over the full history, not a top-N list.
8. **UI only, recorded for reference:**
   - The Route section is all steps: Routes compared → Reel → How we've run similar products → Machine → Tool → Material piece & conversion → Print.
   - The section strip lives in the frozen header.
