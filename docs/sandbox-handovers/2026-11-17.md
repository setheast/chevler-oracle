## Rulings

1. **A long Random Repeat sheet is printed reel-to-reel and cut on the Sheeter** (Seth, 17 Nov 2026; production-knowledge §45).
   - **When it applies:** a Random Repeat sheet longer than the Reel-Sheet's longest repeat (489mm).
   - **The route:** printed on a Reel-Reel press (Large or Small), then cut on the Sheeter, at any length in the Sheeter's range (375–1,500mm).
   - The Main workbook already routed it this way (Reel-Sheet off; Reel-Reel + Sheeter on). Its cut-length lookup only read the repeat list, though, so it returned no cut and the job couldn't be priced. No saved quote depended on that, so no legacy flag was needed.
   - **Registered** print keeps its repeat list, because the cut has to land on the print.
   - **The job (code seed rates, 8% NP):**
     - 600 × 900 is turned to run 900 across the 918 reel and cut at 600 (98% utilisation).
     - Route: Large Reel-Reel → Sheeter → Guillotine → Packing.
     - Price: £160.11 / **£149.34** / £143.96 per 1,000 at 10k / 15k / 20k (£2,240 at 15k).
     - Better sizes offers 570 × 900 (2 across the 1,140, 100%) at about £25 per 1,000 less.
   - **Two consequences for search:**
     - The reel table tries the turned cuts when the sheet auto-rotates, so the table's prices match the form's.
     - Better sizes lists a Sheeter cut once per reel × outs, not once per mm.
   - **For Oracle:** the press's cut-length limit and the Sheeter's limit are both machine data, and the cut rule depends on the print type (Registered = the repeat list; Random past every repeat = the Sheeter range; Plain = the Sheeter range). The real Sheeter limits are still to be confirmed (R28).

2. **Silicone-treated papers can't be printed** (Seth, 17 Nov 2026: "STGP stands for Silicon Treated Greaseproof and cannot be printed on").
   - This covers STGP (Silidor, white and natural) and STVP (silicone vegetable parchment).
   - In the sandbox it is an advisory check, `unprintable-paper` (attention level). It never blocks a quote or changes a price.
   - **For Oracle:** give the material record a printable flag. Don't match on the description, which is what the sandbox does (`UNPRINTABLE_PAPER = /silicone/i`). Route candidacy for printed work should skip unprintable paper.
   - **A related stock fact:** no 34gsm greaseproof is stocked. The lightest GP is 39gsm (mill reels 516 / 918 / 1,140 / 1,315). The only 34gsm line is a 340mm White Grease Resistant reel. A 34gsm GP enquiry is quoted on 39gsm.

3. **Run-history speeds are per stroke of the product's die, not the machine's standard** (Seth, 17 Nov 2026, from YSB508; §39 addendum).
   - **The fault:** Access's run factor is output per hour. The sandbox turned it into strokes a minute by dividing by the machine's standard pieces a stroke (CALMU 1, MUF01-S/L 1), not by the die's.
     - YSB508 (44 × 30 Brim Case on the CALMU 4-up die) read 124/min, but it runs 31 strokes a minute (124 cases a minute).
     - With the die fitted, the form then priced 496 cases a minute.
   - **The divisor now:** RF Wrap's fixed 2-up; otherwise the one ready die for the size on that machine; otherwise the machine's standard. This is the loader's existing rule, now shared by the speed history, size history, similar-product facts and the similar-products panel.
   - **The workbook agrees:** RF Wrap is 10 strokes × 2-up on MUF01-S/L; CALMU 28 × 4; MUF01 twin dies 18.5–26 × 2.
   - **The sweep (2,015 Cases products):** history values change on CALMU 667, MUF01 377, MUF01-S 279, MUF01-L 270, 9REEL 82 (2-up dies, now faster) and ZHEN-TP-15 18. Products with no die on file are unchanged. Recalled quotes keep the speeds stored with them.
   - **For Oracle:** a speed is stored as **strokes per minute**, with the pieces per stroke taken from the die fitted. Output per minute = strokes × pieces per stroke. Never convert an output rate with a machine-wide constant. This is one more reason to treat imported Access speeds as unverified (R14).

4. **An Access BOM mill reel with no length carries its QPA in kg per 1,000, and runs as bought** (Seth, 17 Nov 2026, from YSB508; §39 addendum).
   - **The evidence:** YSB508's 516mm reel (100220) at 0.8901 kg per 1,000 equals WIBA05's 516 × 2,000m reel at 0.01725 reels per 1,000. Both are the same 44 × 30 on the CALMU. The CALMU runs the 516mm mill reel as bought, with no slitting.
   - **The rule:** the loader takes such a line as the reel × 2,000m, with QPA = kg ÷ (width m × 2,000 × gsm ÷ 1,000), and conversion None when Plain.
   - Only 4 of 6,659 products change: YSB508, EW504, WSTE63 and WT504.
   - **For Oracle:** a BOM line's quantity unit must be explicit (kg, reels, m or sheets per 1,000). Never infer it from whether a length is filled in. This is the same lesson as ruling 103's "explicit form, never inferred".

5. **If a number isn't used for costing, don't display it** (Seth, 17 Nov 2026: "if a number isn't being used for costing, why are we displaying it?").
   - Settings → Work Centres no longer shows Machine £, Labour £, Engineer £ or Sheets per cut. Nothing prices from them, and they stay in the params file.
   - **For Oracle:** a rate screen shows the inputs the engine actually reads and the calculation that produced them (ruling 109). Breakdown fields belong to calibration, not to the work-centre form.

6. **Live data fixes are one press: preview, then one audited, rollbackable edit** (pattern, 17 Nov 2026).
   - **The fix:** live still charged the engineer premium on nine work centres (02CUP, 12CUP, SWCUP, ECLAR, VP6-H, MUF01, MUF01-S, MUF01-L, ZHEN-SP-15), against ruling 110. The seeds have had none since 12 Nov.
   - **How it works:** a pure `removeEngineerCharge` sets make-ready = cost rate and engineer 0. It has a dry-run preview and repeat presses do nothing. The box shows only while a row still differs.
   - It is the same shape as the 13 Nov "Book group dies" button. Both now sit with the Tools imports in a closed "Imports (rarely needed)" fold. They are kept because the Tools seed is empty, so they rebuild the list after a lost volume, and the S8 import is how a refreshed Access extract adds new dies.
   - **For Oracle:** a correction to calibration data is a reviewed, logged change set, never a hand edit row by row. Seed Oracle's work centres without any engineer or make-ready premium, so nothing needs porting here.

## Addendum (afternoon, 17 Nov 2026): print origination on Main, live engineer fix verified

**Status:** built and pushed to main 17 Nov 2026 (afternoon). **Not yet folded into Oracle.** Sources: production-knowledge §46 (Seth, 17 Nov 2026) and the build plan phase log "Print origination on Main".

1. **Printed Main products carry print origination (Seth, 17 Nov 2026).** Until today a printed sheet, reel, disc or liner quoted from Main carried no stereo or cylinder cost.
   - **Rule:** the press the job routes to prints from one plate per colour. The Small Reel-Reel *is* the Narrow Reeler and the Large Reel-Reel *is* the Wide Reeler, so Main uses the same press × plate £/colour table as Cases (one source; live: Reel-Sheet and Narrow stereo £200, cylinder £842; Wide cylinder £1,220). The default plate is a stereo where the press takes one; it can be switched per job.
   - **Bases:** upfront (a one-off line, VAT on it, never in the unit price) or amortised **at cost** (cost ÷ (years × annual volume) per unit, inside factory cost but outside the margin gross-up and every uplift; the margin %s are worked on revenue less it — Oracle ruling on tooling / origination at cost, 4 Nov, now applies to Main too). Repeat order = held, no charge.
   - **For Oracle:** origination is a per-job line tied to the Print Tools register by press × plate kind, not a per-estimator feature. The quote must show which press it was priced for; a route change that moves the press must re-price the plates (the sandbox raises `origination-press` when they disagree). Margin figures need an explicit "less at cost" base so GP % and NP % multiply out by hand.
2. **Engineer charge removed on live (Lewys ruling 1 / Oracle ruling 110) — verified.** Seth pressed the fix at 13:20; the live `/api/cases-params` export shows every work centre with make-ready = cost rate and Engineer £ 0, and every cost rate matches the seed. Still not on the S7 card in live Settings: Conversion Costing make-ready for the Sheeter (£50.15 against £62.33) and the Slitter (£32.78 against £45.18).

**What to port:** `MAIN_PLATE_PRESS` / `mainPrintPress` / `mainPlateCount` (`origination.ts`), `amortisedOrigination` and the at-cost step in `engines/main/*` (as a rule — `engines/main/origination.test.ts` is the regression point), and `mainOriginationChecks` (`estimate-checks.ts`).
